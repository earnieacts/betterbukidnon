Business Permits in Bukidnon

Business permits are issued by your city or municipality. Here is the general process.

Issued By

City/Municipal BPLO

Location

Your City or Municipal Hall

Renewal

Every Year, Usually in January

Fees and Taxes

Set by Each LGU

Who issues business permits

The Provincial Government of Bukidnon does not issue business permits for stores, offices or other establishments. Permits (often called the Mayor's Permit) are issued by the Business Permits and Licensing Office (BPLO) of the city or municipality where the business is located: Malaybalay City, Valencia City, or one of the 20 municipalities. Requirements, fees and deadlines vary, so confirm with that office. Find your city or municipality

Getting a New Business Permit

The usual order of steps. Your city or municipality may combine some of them in a Business One-Stop Shop.

Step What to Do Where
1 Register the business Register the business name (sole proprietorship), the corporation or partnership, or the cooperative. DTI, SEC, or CDA
2 Get a barangay business clearance Apply for a clearance from the barangay where the business will operate. Barangay hall
3 Apply for the business permit Submit the application form and requirements. The office assesses your fees and local business taxes. City or municipal BPLO
4 Pay fees and taxes Pay the assessed fees and local business taxes and keep the official receipt. City or municipal treasurer's office
5 Complete clearances and inspections Depending on the business, this can include a fire safety inspection, a sanitary permit, a zoning or locational clearance, and building or occupancy permits. BFP and the city or municipal offices concerned
6 Claim and display the permit Claim the business permit and display it where customers can see it. City or municipal BPLO
7 Register with the BIR Register for tax purposes with the BIR Revenue District Office that covers your business address. BIR

Commonly Asked Requirements

Each city and municipality publishes its own list. Use these to prepare, then confirm with your BPLO.

New Business

  • Duly accomplished Application Form
  • DTI Certificate of Business Name Registration (for sole proprietorship)
  • SEC Registration (for corporation/partnership)
  • CDA Registration (for cooperatives)
  • Barangay Business Clearance
  • Proof of business location, such as a lease contract, or the title or tax declaration if you own the place
  • Valid ID of the owner or authorized person

Renewal

  • Previous year's business permit
  • Declaration or proof of gross sales or receipts for the past year
  • Barangay business clearance for the current year
  • Updated clearances your city or municipality asks for

What the Provincial Government Handles

  • Under the Local Government Code, permits to extract sand, gravel and other quarry resources are issued by the Provincial Governor.
  • Provincial taxes and fees are paid at the Provincial Treasurer's Office. Provincial Treasurer's Office

Frequently Asked Questions

When do I renew my business permit?

Every year. Under the Local Government Code, local business taxes are due in January, with quarterly installments where the LGU allows them. Check your city or municipality's deadline and penalties.

Can I apply online?

Some cities and municipalities accept online applications through their own systems or an electronic Business One-Stop Shop. Ask your BPLO what it offers.

My business has branches in different towns.

Each city or municipality issues its own permit. A branch or outlet usually needs a permit from the LGU where it operates.

Where do I register my business name?

Sole proprietors register the business name with the DTI. Corporations and partnerships register with the SEC, and cooperatives with the CDA.
